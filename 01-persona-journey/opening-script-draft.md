*"I've done everything SCIS told me to do: the internship, the exchange, the projects. Now I just need it to pay off."*

That's Marcus Tan, a Year 4 Information Systems student at SCIS. In Year 3 he did an exchange in Europe and a ten-week data analyst internship at a local bank. This year he's carrying a capstone with an industry sponsor, three modules a term, and a full-time job search, all while commuting about 50 minutes each way from Tampines. He's aiming for a data analyst or associate product manager role.

Marcus plans everything in spreadsheets, and he gets anxious when something is outside his control. He wants his answers in one place, not across five portals. And he trusts seniors on Telegram more than official announcements.

His expectation of SCIS is simple: *"SCIS gets me employed and graduates me on time."*

Here's the loyalty challenge. Marcus's best moment is the capstone showcase, when a sponsor invites him to interview. That's a plus two. But his worst moment is the final-term graduation audit, a minus two. A late audit flags a missing requirement, the module he needs is already full, and his job offer depends on graduating.

So the last big thing SCIS does for Marcus is the thing he remembers. Loyalty matters even though he's leaving: graduates who feel the promise was kept hire from SCIS, mentor juniors, recommend the school and sponsor capstones. ## Likely Q&A for this section

- **Why a Year 4 persona?** He's at the point where the promise (employability) is tested, and the point where we can still change the outcome.
- **Why is the final audit the worst moment?** It hits his two biggest risks at once: not graduating and losing the job offer. It also comes late, with little time to fix it. It's a "moment of truth" in Carlzon's sense.
- **Why does loyalty matter for a student who is graduating?** Alumni hire, mentor, recommend and sponsor. This links to the "Promote" stage from W11.
